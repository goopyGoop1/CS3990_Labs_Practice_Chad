About Canada

  • Canada is the second-largest country in the world by land area, covering about 9.98 million square kilometers.
  • Ice hockey is the most popular sport, and Canada has won more Olympic gold medals in hockey than any other country.
  • Canada produces about 85% of the world's maple syrup, mainly from Quebec.
  • The famous dish poutine (fries, cheese curds, and gravy) was invented in the 1950s in rural Quebec.
  • The coldest temperature recorded in Canada was -63°C (-81.4°F) in Snag, Yukon, while the highest was 45°C (113°F) in Saskatchewan.
  • It has the longest coastline in the world, stretching over 202,080 km.

Canada is the second-largest country in the world by land area, known for its stunning natural landscapes, diverse culture, and strong economy.
It is a bilingual nation with English and French as its official languages.
Canada is home to vibrant cities like Toronto, Vancouver, and Montreal, as well as breathtaking natural wonders such as the Rocky Mountains, Niagara Falls, and the Northern Lights.
The country is renowned for its high quality of life, universal healthcare system, and progressive social policies.
With a rich history shaped by Indigenous cultures, European settlers, and global immigration, Canada embraces multiculturalism and is known for its welcoming and inclusive society.

Canada is made up of 10 provinces and 3 territories, each with unique geography and culture. The provinces, such as Ontario, Quebec, and British Columbia, have more autonomy and are responsible for their own governance. The three territories—Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut—are largely governed by the federal government due to their lower populations and vast northern landscapes. From the Atlantic coastline of Newfoundland to the Rocky Mountains of Alberta, each region contributes to Canada's rich diversity.

Alberta is a province in western Canada, known for its vast landscapes and natural resources.
It is home to the Rocky Mountains, the prairies, and the oil sands, making it a key player in Canada's economy.
The capital city is Edmonton, while Calgary is the largest city and a major business hub.
Alberta is famous for Banff and Jasper National Parks, attracting millions of tourists every year.
The province has a rich cultural heritage, with Indigenous communities, ranching traditions, and a strong energy sector.
